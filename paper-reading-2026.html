- Consensus
  + link. "Cadence: Extreme Pipelining with Multiple Concurrent Proposers" by Babel et al.
  + Geo-decentralization
    * link. "The Geography of (Concurrent) Block Proposing" by Maollemi et al.
    Closest to the Toly intuition of "if you have multiple information sources and MCP, you incentivize people to distribute geographically. Model different information sources a independent GBMs. Model the game as an auction where exclusive access to information is rewarded whereas common information is not.
    * link. "On the Geographic Incentives of Multiple Concurrent Proposers" by Babel et al.
    * link. "The Price of Decentralization in Block Building" by Öz et al.
  + Rationality and consensus
    * link. "Demystifying Incentives in the Consensus Computer" by Luu et al.
    The Verifier's Dilemma paper. Argues that if the cost of verifying transactions is high, it might be rational to skip verification, as long as other miners are as well. The end result is a chain with unvalidated txns. The gas limit fixes this, as does some honesty assumption on the number of attesters in PoS. One interesting angle that we do see today however is that some transactions might not be worth including in the block simply because the fees are too low (esp. during periods of higher volatility).
    * link. "BAR Nash Equilibrium and Application to Blockchain Design" by Reynouard et al.
    * link. "An economic model of consensus on distributed ledgers" by Halaburda et al.
    * link. "Rational Consensus" by Halpern and Vilaça
  + Timing games
    * link. "Designing Incentives for Responsive Consensus Protocols" by Kelkar et al.
    * link. "Time is Money: Strategic Timing Games in Proof-of-Stake Protocols" by Schwarz-Schilling et al.
  + Mining games
    * link. "Unruly by Design: Fee Volatility and Strategic Attacks in Bitcoin Mining" by Schär et al.
    * link. "When Higher Stakes Weaken Security" by Pablo D. Azar and Maryam Farboodi
    Analyzes the probability of a fork arising from increases in fees. Empirical econ paper that demonstrates that higher fees imply higher uncle rates. Interesting that the fed is looking at this.

- MEV/block building/execution layer
  + link. "On Bitcoin and Red Balloons" by Babaioff et al.
  + PBS auctions
    * link. "Just-in-Time Resale in an Ahead-of-Time Auction for Faster Execution" by Öz et al.
    * link. "From Competition to Centralization: The Oligopoly in Ethereum Block Building Auctions" by Wu et al.
    * link. "Strategic Bidding Wars in On-chain Auctions" by Wu et al.
    * link. "Decentralization of Ethereum's Builder Market" by Yang et al.
    * link. "Competing Auctions in Intermediated Markets" by Mazorra et al.
    * link. "Becoming Immutable: How Ethereum is Made" by Canidio & Pahari      
    * link. "From PBS to ePBS: the Microstructure of Block Building" by Liu et al.
  + PropAMMs
    * link. "Active Liquidity On Chain: Evidence from PropAMMs Across Chains" by Solmaz et al.

- Dynamic mechanism design
  + link. "Optimal selling time with evolving private information" by Kiho Yoon
    Single item sale, one buyer, evolving private valuation. Determines the optimal selling time of the item. With assumptions, shows that this is implementable in the dynamic setting.
  + link. "Timing of Auctions of Real Options" by Lin William Cong
    Studies selling options to a set of buyers who have random and private cost of exercising. The key point is that when the seller can determine when to run the auction, they push it later to reduce the window of time post-auction for the buyer to exercise it. But if the buyer can initiate the auction, the auction happens much earlier.
